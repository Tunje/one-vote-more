# v11 chronicles: analysis

Three century games (seeds 1, 2 and 3) on the v11 defaults, commit f411544, 16 November 2026. Beside them, three
century games on the same code with only the v10 switches on, to separate tonight's every-game changes from the v11
pieces.

## The short version, for listening

Version eleven is the first version where family farms and bakeries are part of every game, where food, building
materials and freight follow the eight rules, and where campaigns follow rivalries. It also carries the rulings of the
last day in every game: credit follows what a firm is going to sell and never a ceiling made of its past sales, public
companies borrow like any other firm, and fines and union demands read the year.

Over a hundred years, two of the three colonies end far larger than in version ten, and one ends smaller. Seed one
ends with twenty-one thousand nine hundred adults against seven thousand eight hundred in version ten: nearly three
times as many. Seed three ends with ten thousand nine hundred against seven thousand two hundred. Seed two ends with
nine thousand nine hundred against sixteen thousand two hundred. Added together, the three colonies hold forty-two
thousand seven hundred adults against thirty-one thousand two hundred.

People are better fed. There is no famine in any of the three games; version ten had one in seed one and one in
seed three. Shortages of goods of every kind are recorded one hundred and fifty times in seed one against eighteen
hundred in version ten, and fourteen hundred times in seed three against two thousand. Seed two goes the other way:
eleven hundred against two hundred and forty. Family farms number ninety-five to one hundred and forty per colony at
the end, and big farms stand beside them: eighty, twenty-seven and forty-two.

Governments are less hated. Approval averages forty-five per cent in all three games against thirty-four to
thirty-eight in version ten, and it never falls below twenty-five per cent in any year. In version ten it did in
eight to fourteen years per game. Approval still slides gently from about fifty in the first decade to the low
forties late on, as Ella predicted.

The weak point is work. Unemployment is low for twenty years and then climbs and stays high. The century averages are
twenty-five, thirty-seven and thirty-four per cent, against twenty-four, twenty-two and thirty-three in version ten.
In seeds two and three it is above forty per cent for half a century. The colonies grow in people faster than they
grow in jobs. Real income is nevertheless higher in two of the three games.

The credit ruling, taken alone, makes the colony poorer. On the same code with only the version ten switches on, seed
one ends with five thousand adults where the old version ten had seven thousand eight hundred, and seed three with six
thousand six hundred against seven thousand two hundred, with real income falling to seventy and one hundred by the
end. So what lifts version eleven is its own pieces, above all food and family farms, and they lift it in spite of
the credit change. Why the credit change costs so much is the first thing Bob measures today: his guess and mine is
that the bank's small lending room is now in demand from every firm, including the public companies, and runs out.

Three things to carry into the day. First, the unemployment after year twenty is the main problem of this version.
Second, the credit ruling is right in principle and is hurting in practice, most likely because the bank cannot lend
enough; your ruling on the bank is not built yet. Third, the builders rules and the planning stage are not in:
builders set off a price spiral with materials, and the planning stage turned from a gain into a loss once the credit
change was in.

## What v11 switches on
On top of v10: E160 and E162 (family farms and bakeries), R8 with R8_FREIGHT, R8_MATERIAL and R8_FOOD (depots follow
full trucks; buyers bid what an input or food is worth to them, no price rules; farms follow their sales), TREND2,
RULE_COST3 and RIVALRY (approval moves only outside its 12-month range, a lower cost of ruling, campaigns aim by
rivalry).

In every game since 16 November, whatever the switches: credit lines, input budgets and working-capital loans rest on
expected sales (the production plan, signed contracts, the forecast), with the borrower's record in place of the
profit test; reserves read the year and do not hold inputs back; public enterprises borrow like any firm; builders'
orders count their supplier credit; fines and union demands read the year; a stuck public project is re-tendered at
most once a year; laws of switched-off features are no longer party commitments.

Not in v11: the builders rules (R8_BUILDERS), the planning stage (E165, E165B), housing under the eight rules,
rescues and half-built takeovers, freight on real roads with road works, private roads, road safety, siting and town
grids, ecology and seasons, delegation, the Minister of Agriculture, the hiring market, the three crops.

## What works
- **Food.** No famine in any v11 game (v10: one each in seeds 1 and 3; the v10 switches on tonight's code: one each
  in seeds 1 and 3). Shortage events for all goods together, from the yearly counts: 151, 1,109 and 1,436, against
  1,862, 238 and 2,062 in v10 and 3,637 and 2,972 with the v10 switches on tonight's code (seeds 1 and 3). The food
  reserve was opened 5, 4 and 12 times (v10: 7, 7, 6). Family farms at year 100: 140, 95 and 128; family
  bakeries 28, 22 and 39; big farms 80, 27 and 42. The flood of family farms that starved the colony on 15 November
  does not happen: farms follow their sales and families found only while orders exceed what is grown.
- **Growth.** Every colony grows in every decade. Seed 1 triples v10's size and becomes independent in year 48.
- **Politics.** Approval between 41 and 55 in every decade of every game; no year under 25%. No confidence votes
  passed: none (v10: 2, 1, 1). Opposition parties publicly questioned government decisions 67, 48 and 125 times
  (lines in the logs).
- **Public debt** stays small: a peak of 13% of GDP on seed 1, none on the others (v10 seed 1 peaked at 110%).

## What is wrong
1. **Unemployment after year 20.** It passes 30% in the third or fourth decade and stays there: 33, 78 and 70 years at
   30% or more. People arrive and are born faster than workplaces appear. Of the new private firms that opened, 46%,
   29% and 30% were working twelve months later (192 of 415, 103 of 354, 123 of 405).
2. **Seed 2.** It ends 6,000 adults short of v10 with unemployment above 40% for sixty years. It has one freight depot
   at the end (seed 1 has three) and two working service hubs with 63 mothballed. The bank failed once and there were
   three credit crunches in that game. Not yet explained.
3. **Service hubs.** 71, 63 and 51 mothballed at year 100 against 20, 2 and 31 running: Ella's finding that people
   have little left after essentials, and that a hub without its specialist makes nothing.
4. **Insolvencies doubled** on seeds 1 and 3 (525 and 440). With more credit, more firms are started and more fail.
   Jobs lost in closures exceed jobs in new firms at twelve months on every seed (5,929 against 3,486; 2,068 against
   1,280; 10,969 against 2,238).
5. **Family grant programmes still often end with nobody taking them**, and a few grant-funded family firms failed
   (3, 7 and 4 mentions in the logs). Both are now public facts the opposition can use.
6. **The bank.** One bank failure each on seeds 2 and 3. Its lending room is capital times ten and nothing else; the
   user's ruling (lend against deposits, be able to raise capital) is not built.

## What the 30-year tests said, and did not say
On the code with the credit change, ten seeds over 30 years: v11 4,875 adults, unemployment 18.3%, food shortfall
13.9 permille, real wage 3.01, against 4,487, 20.9%, 19.7 and 2.52 for the v10 switches (planning/ab-v11.md). The
century games agree on food, real income and size for two seeds of three. They add what 30 years cannot show: the
unemployment that builds after year 20, and seed 2.

On seed 2 the picture is the reverse of seeds 1 and 3: the v11 pieces themselves cost 4,400 adults against the same
code without them (9,926 against 14,311), on top of the 1,900 the every-game changes cost against the old v10. So the

better one. With only the version ten switches, seed two's bank fails five times and has seventeen credit crunches in
the century, which fits the reading that the credit change overloads the bank.
