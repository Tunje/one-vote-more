# Second review of the rebuild plan: the whole, from each perspective

Reviews of [rebuild-company.md](rebuild-company.md) version 8 (commit dfab228). The user: "asked them to review the
whole thing from their perspectives not just their parts". Collected by Wilma, 2026-11-17, as each session sent it,
shortened only in wording.

## Maja: seen from the people

She read all 15 sections and checked the old household code for what "taken wholesale" carries with it.

### 1. Does it hold together?

The company side holds. It breaks at the households, because the old household code is not neutral:

a) **It is keyed by the six districts.** Pools, the age tables and the job allocation are all per district (71 uses in
   the four files). "No sectors beyond landfallpoint" removes the thing they are keyed by, and homes per 100 m cell with
   rent per home sit against one account per block and band. The households cannot fit in until what replaces the
   districts is decided, and it has to be decided for the pools first.
b) **Self-employment is a producer outside the company.** The pools hold sole proprietors who enter from unemployment
   and sell services. That breaks "ONE kind of thing that produces" and "no services item on a market". They have to
   become family companies or go.
c) **The household code reaches into 22 other old economy parts** (food rules, service rules, shops, comfort, trade,
   the market, commuting). Each reach has to be cut. If comfort, shops and business services are gone, the households'
   comfort and services budgets have no seller, and demand for services (what rules 7 and 8 read) is zero.
d) **The numbers.** The plan says the household side carries over and also that the old data file's numbers do not. The
   household side reads 49 of those numbers, among them what a person needs to eat, in the OLD units.
e) **It contains hard limits:** price floors and ceilings, a ceiling on the self-employed, the 130% occupancy ceiling.
   "Wholesale" carries them in unmentioned, against "No hard floors or ceilings of our own".

### 2. What goes wrong first

**Food in month one, then nobody to sell to.** No private companies are placed and none of the government's
subsidiaries makes food. A farm needs rule 7, a plan, a builder's quote and wait, months of building (and the builders
need material from a maker that does not exist yet), then hiring. Imports are a backstop only on a ship, with the full
round trip, and ships are build step 12. The reserve is undecided. So the settlers' savings buy nothing to eat for
months, and the household code answers hunger with deaths, fewer births and families leaving. At the other end, almost
everyone is jobless in month one, so the benefit is the only income and household demand, the signal every rule reads,
is near zero.

### 3. Against a ruling

- "The bracket is that service's price", set by the price rules, against "Schools and charge it's a bracket in taxes":
  a bracket is a lever the government sets, not a price a company moves. The plan says both. Her opinion: the service's
  rules propose the bracket and the government decides it.
- Self-employed producers against "ONE kind of thing that produces".
- The wholesale hard limits against "No hard floors or ceilings of our own".

### 4. Cannot be built as written, or said two ways

- The household numbers carry over, and the old data file's numbers do not.
- Clinic to deaths and school to skill are in the household side; the plan also lists them as open. Childcare and elder
  care were elsewhere and truly are open.
- The starting salary: "area" is undefined without districts, and it is not said whether housing and food are per
  household or per worker. A household has about 1.3 earners, so a per-household cost paid to each worker overpays by
  about a third. Her opinion: per worker.
- "Workers leave for better pay": the old labour market only fills posts from the jobless. Changing jobs is new code.
- The owner taken to court: the owner of a family company is a pool of households with one account, not a person, so
  the pool's other households would pay the debt. Her opinion: a separate account for the owner inside the pool.
- The purse has money out to state companies and grants but none to people: the benefit and pensions are on no line.
- Arrivals and departures happen only on ships, and ships are step 12, so every test game before that is a closed
  colony: no settlers, and the jobless and homeless cannot leave.

### 5. Missing from the whole

- What a person does with no work and no money: with self-employment gone and ships late, nothing is left.
- **The first homes:** who owns the homes the settlers land in, how many, at what rent. Without them everyone is beyond
  130% occupancy on day one.
- **The settlers themselves at month one:** how many, their ages, skills and savings.
- Benefits, pensions and the retirement age on the purse.
- Who decides a state service's size: rule 5 on its bracket money, or the government commissioning. Both are written.
- Closing a public school or clinic a place still needs: the rules, or the government.
- Which of the new government acts become cases on the decision desk.
- Until commuting is decided, a plant can hire only those living in its own area.

### 6. The build order

- Households (step 3) need food to buy and homes to live in, but the market is step 4 and homes step 7.
- Arrivals and departures by ship should come with step 3, even if goods by ship wait.
- Benefits and pensions should come with step 3.
- Public services should come with the work-slot types of step 4; until then nobody is served and the household code
  reads that as poor clinic access and more deaths.
- What replaces the districts blocks step 3 itself; it cannot wait for step 13.

(Maja's later correction: "about 1.3 earners per household" and "overpays by about a third" are her estimates, not
measured. The point stands without the figure: the ruling does not say per household or per worker.)

## Terra: seen from space

### 1. Does it hold together?

Mostly, except in one place: **"where"**. "No sectors beyond landfallpoint" removes the districts, and at least seven
sections still stand on a district or an "area": the salary per district and the starting salary "in the area";
households counted and aged per area; a police station's clients; pollution by area; the road network, which routes
district to district; and politics, which elects by district. The plan lists what replaces the districts as one open
point among 23. It is the ground under all of them and has to be decided first. Her opinion: "the area" is the
population's own areas, which already grow out of where homes are built; a district (for seats and for routing) is a
group of areas the government draws as the colony spreads.

The second break is **"a square"**. If a square holds one full size of company of ANY type, one shop in a street block
stops the landlord there from growing, and the landfall tile holds one large company in total. The ruling reads
naturally as competitors of the same kind. Her opinion: one full size PER TYPE.

### 2. What goes wrong first

The builders and material in month one, then the space between plants. Five builders need three months of supplies;
none of the government's subsidiaries makes material, food or components; no private company is placed. So the
builders cannot start, nothing is built, and food has no maker. Imports need a ship and the full round trip. Then every
new plant goes onto a new tile and needs a road before anything moves, and roads are builders' work behind the same
five builders. She expects the first test to show a colony squeezed onto the landfall tile, waiting for builders.

### 3. Against a ruling

- The 50-home block, the 0.85 and 1.15 form factors and the reach figures are old balancing or her old guesses; "not
  their code or balancing" means they are set new.
- Her seven opt-in systems cannot wait switched off: each is part of a step, on, or gone.

### 4. Cannot be built as written

- A plant stands on a set of cells or tiles, not one: a block of homes is 6–7 cells, a farm's fields span a tile.
- A salary per district against no districts.
- The roads route between district anchors; shipping from plant to buyer is new work, or the routing goes area to area.
- A buy-out "to do with land" needs land that someone owns. The plan prices land only for homes and owns land nowhere.
- Noise arrives at step 11, so home values and rents jump then.

### 5. Missing from the whole

- **Land ownership:** who owns an empty tile or cell, how a plan gets it, what a buy-out buys.
- **The landing:** what the first ship brings (people, food, material, components, trucks) and where it stands. With no
  private makers that stock is the whole economy until the first plants open.
- **Roads in month one:** with no districts there is no network.
- **Distance in the labour market:** without travel time a worker takes a job across the colony. Her opinion: compare
  pay after commute time and fare.
- Distance to a service's clients: the town grid already has reaches (school 1.5 km, clinic 2 km, workplace 3 km).
- **Elections:** with districts gone the Assembly's seats have no constituency. Nothing names it.
- The planet acting on companies: storms closing roads and damaging plants, the tidal surge, mud in the freight queue,
  wildlife.
- Where the port is.
- **What the player sees:** the map and town view drew everything from the old plant table. A run-down block should be
  visible, or the player cannot see why rents fell.

### 6. The build order

- Decide before step 1: what replaces the districts, land, the landing, the first roads.
- Homes at step 7 are too late: households are pools per housing block and the salary needs a rent at step 3.
- Freight at step 8 is too late for building at step 6 as soon as plants stand off the landfall tile.
- Seasons and farm yield before food enters the market at step 4.
- A plain map that shows plants belongs at step 2: it is how the user sees whether placement works.

## Ella: seen from politics and from services

### 1. Does it hold together?

a) **Pools against people.** Households are pools holding counts; the company's staff are "the actual people". A pool
   has no persons to put in a company. Union membership and its fee "from every person", the 2/3 of "their normal
   salary", changing jobs and deaths at work all need a person, or a per-company count inside a pool.
b) **Districts.** The whole of politics is built on them: seats, district politics, campaign targeting, pork, the vote
   model, crime, police areas. The electoral map is the bigger dependency and no section names it.
c) **Who moves a tax bracket.** The rules would move a bracket every month, but a bracket is a law: the legislature
   votes it, parties promise it, the government is blamed for it. As written both move it.
d) **Benefits and pensions** are money in for households and on no line of the government's purse. They are the levers
   voters feel most.
e) **What households spend on:** comfort, services and newspapers may have no seller.
f) **Grants:** "all three grant types open" against four listed. Only the user can say which count is right.

### 2. What goes wrong first

Hunger in month one, then no material. The old start's food, mine, material, components and service companies were all
private or co-op. The first farm opens after a full ship round trip plus its building time. In politics that is a
famine emergency in year one of every game, and it would mask everything else.

### 3. Against a ruling

- Three grant types against four.
- "Subsidiaries as before" no longer gives what the old start gave, which was food. Facts only; the user decides.
- One price per company means the old prices per district that voters' cost of living read are gone. A price index has
  to be worked out, or voters lose prices, which no ruling asked for.

### 4. Cannot be built as written

- From step 1 to step 12 the 367 open calls need a stand-in answer and the plan names none. What politics plays with
  for twelve steps is undefined.
- Month one is built at step 2, but grants come at step 9, households at 3 and ships at 12.
- A truck is an item with a quality in two sections and an open point in a third.
- A shutdown is hidden from the rules, but rule 8 also reads whether the work is covered; if a struck plant does not
  count, rivals are founded during every long strike. Her opinion: it counts as covered.

### 5. Missing from the whole

- **The landfall cargo:** stocks, the government's starting money, the settlers' savings.
- **The government's lever list.** Politics plays with levers: the minimum wage, union rights, public pay, benefit and
  pension rates, rent and price controls, import tariffs. Each is kept with a new effect or gone; parties' positions,
  the AI government, promises and the legislature all read the list.
- **Blame:** every ending, layoff, shutdown and famine needs a fact saying who is to blame and where, or voters cannot
  punish anyone.
- **Parties as buyers and owners:** campaign money (ads, the party office, party staff) and a party-owned company's
  profit reaching the party.
- **Public pay:** whether the government can set its own staff's pay above what the rules give.
- **Who pays when a household cannot pay for a service.**

### 6. The build order

- Ships before building: they are the only way material reaches the first builders, and they carry migration.
- Test households and the market together.
- Grants with building, not with the purse.
- From step 1 politics needs stand-ins that say "no unions", "no budget lines", not missing calls.
- Districts and the electoral map have no step.

## Otto: seen from what it takes to get anything built

### 1. Does it hold together?

a) **Nothing can be built in month one.** Five builders, no maker of material or components, no farm; a builder needs
   three months of supplies before it starts; the only other source is a ship round trip. The first plant of any kind,
   including the mine and material plant that would end the shortage, waits for a ship.
b) **"Only price moves price" against the builders.** A builder whose crews wait for material has empty work slots. The
   plan names only freight, the road and the buyer's money as causes that are not price. As written, crews waiting for
   material lower the builder's price and output, lead rule 6 to cut it, and lead to sales death. That is how the old
   builders died: on seed 1, 60 of 103 builder deaths were waiting for stock.
c) **Buildings changing hands.** Growing by buy-out, "a built cell keeps its building", and "buy another building and
   continue there" together mean a building changes owner. The plan never says whether a building belongs to a type
   (can a bakery's building become a mine's), whether a bought plant is rebuilt or kept, or what happens to a dead
   company's building.
d) **The square against homes.** A street block "holds one full size of company" and a block of 50 homes takes 6–7 of
   its 16 cells. The two measure land differently.
e) A plan is not a company and so has no money field, yet it pays a builder and takes a grant.

### 2. What goes wrong first

Material, then components, in month one. If month one places their makers, the next bottleneck is the same as before:
material output against the builders' three-month stock, with idle crews read as a price signal. His opinion: month one
needs the makers of material and components and a food producer, whoever owns them.

### 3. Against a ruling

- Three grant types against four: three only if farms and bakeries are one family grant.
- "No private companies placed" against "5 building companies": the plan does not say whether the public works
  department is one of the five, and "alot of oppretunity" reads as work waiting for them.

### 4. Cannot be built as written

- The hold-back is defined for stock only; for a builder, landlord or service the plan does not say what is offered
  and what is held back.
- Step 3 cannot be built before what replaces the districts is decided.
- Whether a plant keeps running while it is built a size up.
- What upkeep uses: if material, it competes with building for the same scarce good.
- A landlord with blocks in two places has one rent moved by all its empty homes together.

### 5. Missing from the whole

- **Material to the building site:** who carries the three months of supplies, and to where.
- A new plant's road, power and water connections (charged for homes only).
- **Land for plants.**
- The order of jobs in a builder's book.
- **Empty buildings after an ending:** stand, decay, sold, demolished.
- **Homes at landfall:** how many, what form, whose.

### 6. The build order

- Freight at step 8 comes after the market, building and homes have been tuned on goods that move without trucks.
  "That is the 'tested against a game that did not contain the others' failure of section 1."
- Ships must come before building, or month one must place the makers.
- The landfall homes with a rent at step 2 or 3.
- The purse at step 9 is after building, but the state is the builders' main client in month one.
- Until endings are built at step 10 nothing dies, so the reports of steps 4 to 9 will not show the deaths that were
  the old failure. His opinion: report "would have ended here" from step 4 on.

## Bob: seen from markets, money, credit and freight

### 1. Does it hold together? "Not yet."

a) **Month one leaves the chains without a first link.** Nobody makes food, mineral, material or components. A family
   farm under a grant is a plan that needs a builder, and the builder needs material that only a built plant can make.
   "That is a deadlock." The old game got past it with placed private farms, a mine and a material plant, plus starting
   stock and money.
b) **Salaries have nothing to start from:** no food price and no "area" in month one.
c) **Salaries can fall without end.** The lowering rule fires whenever applicants exceed posts, rule 9 only breaks a
   tie, and there are no floors. Under lasting unemployment salaries fall every month, household orders and then prices
   follow: a deflation spiral. The starting salary only matters in a tie month.
d) **Held-back stock keeps a plant producing at full.** Held-back stock is nobody's signal and rule 4 reads only output
   that did not sell, so a producer below its making price holds stock back while nothing cuts its output. It piles up
   stock for up to two years until sales death, and the money in that stock leaves circulation.
e) **Hunger can be invisible.** A household that cannot afford food buys less and never posts the order, and an order
   stopped by the buyer's money is not counted as unfilled. Hunger from poverty then reads as zero.
f) **Household money has nowhere to go** if comfort, shops and services have no seller: forced saving, and demand
   drains away.

### 2. What goes wrong first

Food and material in months 1 to 6. If that is patched, the money circuit: the state pays wages, benefits and pensions
from month one but taxes come at step 9, so the treasury drains or is bottomless. After that freight, exactly as in v12.

### 3. Against a ruling

- "No private companies placed" has to exempt the five builders, four of which were private.
- Three grant types against four.
- The informal sector and the self-employed produce outside any company.
- "Only pay moves a salary" carries no quote and no proposal mark; if it is Wilma's reading it should say so.

### 4. Cannot be built as written

- Staff as actual people against households as pools.
- Rule 5 says build a new plant; section 5 says grow a size first. Which does the rule run.
- **The making price is never defined:** which costs count, at what output. Rule 9, the hold-back, sales death and the
  salary line all hinge on it. At low sales the fixed costs per unit become huge, which flips rule 9 to "climbing has
  priority" exactly when a firm cannot sell and pushes it into sales death.
- Whether an export counts as sold, and whether stock waiting at the port is unsold.
- A tax bracket and an approved utility price put a second owner on a price the rules own.
- Imports cut off too early: one family farm covering 2% of demand stops all food imports.
- Nothing pauses the two-year sales-death clock or the idle time during a shutdown.

### 5. Missing from the whole

- The definition of the making price.
- Where money comes from and goes: the treasury, the bank and the households at month one; what enters and leaves on
  ships.
- The interest rate: who sets it, by which rule.
- The size and payer of the benefit and pensions.
- Saving for replacement: ending 2 ("can't afford to replace them") needs it.
- Land value for plants.
- **The order of the month:** when trade, production, the rules, wages and rent happen, and which month's signals the
  rules read.
- The time and cost of switching to the second output.
- A price index for real income.
- What stands at landfall.

### 6. The build order

- Bank accounts at step 2; lending can stay at 5.
- The state's money in and out at step 3, or every test from 3 to 8 measures a world with a free treasury.
- The existing homes and their rent at step 3.
- Freight right after the market and before building.
- The end of a company with the bank or with building; otherwise companies cannot die and the bank lends with no
  default.
- Grants with the plan stage.
- The ship schedule and the import backstop at step 2 or 3; exports can stay late.

### His opinions on open points

No fixed cap on a month's price change, the change in proportion to its signal and the same both ways; twelve months
idle for rule 6; the carrier posts a price per route by the same three rules and the sale is made before carrying; a
short good is shared pro rata among buyers at the same price; "area" is the town the plant or home stands in.
