# Seat 3: In parliament (you lead your party in opposition)

Wilma's proposal for the user, 2026-11-16. Nothing in it is decided or built unless it says "today".
Companion files: `design-seat-1-first-minister.md`, `design-seat-2-cabinet-partner.md`.

## When you sit here

Your party has seats and is not in the government. Today the office then shows one line, "You don't hold the
government's office right now. (The opposition desk comes later.)", and the computer governs. The one thing built
for this seat is the opposition panel: inquiries and legal challenges, from what your party can see.

## The idea in one line

You decide nothing and you can say anything. Your job is to be the government people would choose instead.

## What you do

1. **Question.** Each month you may put questions to ministers on what is going wrong: a shortage, an overrun, a
   scandal. A good question forces an answer on the record, makes news, and costs the minister standing. A question
   with nothing behind it costs you.
2. **Inquire and challenge.** (Today.) Open an inquiry into a decision, or challenge it in court where the law gives
   you a case.
3. **Shadow.** Name one of your members to follow each ministry. A good shadow gets you better information on that
   ministry, sharper questions, and a credible minister on the day you govern. It also gives your ambitious members
   something to hold.
4. **Vote, and trade your votes.** A government without a majority needs outside votes for each bill. You can back
   a bill, oppose it, or offer your votes for a change to it. A government with a majority does not need you, and
   then your vote is only a statement.
5. **Propose.** Put your own bills. They mostly fail; what they do is show what you would do, and sometimes force
   the government to copy you.
6. **Bring the government down.** Move a confidence vote. It needs 76 against. Called at the right moment (a
   partner wavering, a scandal, a broken promise to a supporter) it ends the government; called at the wrong one it
   rallies them and makes you look weak.
7. **Build for the election.** Candidates, money, unions and interest groups, the campaign. This part exists
   today and matters most from this seat.
8. **Deal.** Talk to the government's partners and supporters. A partner who is unhappy is your way back.

## What you cannot do

- Order, spend, appoint, or answer for the colony.
- See the government's papers. You know what is public, what your shadows and your contacts find out, what the
  press prints, and what leaks.

## What lands on the desk

The same room is wrong for this seat: the First Minister's office belongs to someone else now. I propose a smaller
party-leader's office in the assembly building, with the same objects meaning different things:
- **Paper:** the press, as now. It matters more here; it is where you fight.
- **In-tray:** your shadows' reports on each ministry, and your party's own reports (polls, money, members).
- **Inbox:** party staff, members, unions, donors.
- **Red phone:** other party leaders, in and out of government.
- **Calendar:** the assembly's sittings, question days, votes coming up, the election.
- **Door:** your members, and visitors who want something from a future government.
- **Window:** the colony, as now.

## The pressure on you

- **Nothing to hand out.** In government you keep your party with posts. Here you have only shadow posts and the
  promise of winning. This is when a challenger is most likely.
- **Being ignored.** A government doing well makes the opposition invisible. Your standing decays if you do
  nothing, so each month has to be spent on something.
- **More than one opposition party.** You compete with the others out of government for the same attention and
  the same voters. The largest is "the opposition"; the rest have to work harder to be heard.

## How you leave this seat

- **Up, to seat 1 or 2:** an election; or the government falls and you are in the next one.
- There is no "down" from here except losing.

## How you lose the game from here

- Your party takes no seat at an election.
- Your party removes you: after a lost election, or after a long time out with no prospect of getting back.

## What exists today that this stands on

- The opposition panel (inquiries and legal challenges), the campaign, the press, scandals and their revival.
- Votes in the assembly, governments that fall, extra elections, confidence votes counted at 76.
- Members with ambitions and grievances, challengers and a contest.

## What would have to be built

- Questions as an action with a cost and an effect.
- Shadows: members assigned to ministries, and what they learn.
- Vote trading with a computer government, and bills from the opposition.
- A confidence vote the player can call.
- The party-leader's office and the routing of what lands on it.
- The ending: a screen for each of the two ways to lose.

## Open questions for you

1. Should every party out of government play the same way, or does only the largest get "leader of the
   opposition" (first questions, more attention)?
2. Is there a threshold for getting any seat at all, or can you be reduced to one member and fight back?
3. How much should the opposition see? The less it sees, the harder and the more realistic; the more, the more
   there is to do.
4. Do you want the separate room, or the same office with different contents?
