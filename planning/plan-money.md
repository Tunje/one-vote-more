# Money supply (A61, 2026-11-13)

User ruling: "On banking I like both, but for independence currency there needs to be negotiating after severing
connection, also it's not a closed loop: export goods back to the homeworld adds money."

Background (Bob's money-stock check, seed 1): total money +66% over 50 years while the population grew 3.6×, so wages
and prices halved. Money enters only from off-world. The treasury idle-money rule was tried and shelved (draining the
reserve backfired); the binding job limit is supply (services sold out), being tested econ-only (E134).

## M1 — the open loop first: trade is the money channel (Bob)
- Money enters with export earnings, migrants' savings and charter funding; it leaves with imports, charter dues/taxes
  to the home world, profits sent home and emigrants. Net exports grow the money stock.
- First: a money-flow ledger by channel per year (in: exports, migrants, charter, other; out: imports, dues, remitted
  profits, emigrants). Find why net inflow is small (imports eating exports? dues? freight?).
- Then make exports a real political lever: export promotion, export tax, home-world tariffs and quotas (the home world
  can squeeze the colony by restricting imports of its goods — leverage in the independence struggle).

## M2 — bank credit creation (Bob)
- The colony bank creates deposits when it lends (fractional reserve), so money grows with borrowing.
- Lending limited by a reserve ratio and capital; interest rate set by the bank (later the central bank).
- Brings credit booms and busts: bad loans in a slump, bank runs, a bank failure, bailouts — political fights
  (Anni: banking law, deposit guarantee; Wilma: bailout bills).

## M3 — own currency after independence (Wilma: home world / independence; Bob: monetary mechanics)
- Under the charter the colony uses the home world's money (it cannot print).
- Independence does not settle it: after severing, a NEGOTIATION with the home world follows (Wilma, home_world.gd):
  - keep the home currency (currency union: no printing, trade stays easy) or issue a colony currency;
  - conversion of savings and bank deposits; who keeps the reserves held at home;
  - the colony's debts to the home world and home-world firms (repay, reschedule, repudiate);
  - trade terms (tariffs, ship access) as the home world's bargaining chip; recognition.
  - Failure modes: frozen reserves, a trade embargo, a currency crisis; each a political event with blame.
- With its own currency: a central bank (independence of it is a law), money issue, inflation target, exchange rate
  with the home currency; printing money to fund deficits becomes possible and causes inflation.
